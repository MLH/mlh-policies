# HackSaigon Community Values

**A note on independence:** HackSaigon is an independent group. We are not affiliated with, sponsored by, or related to Major League Hacking (MLH) in any way. We admire the standards MLH has set for student hackathons, and we choose to uphold values inspired by them so that the HackSaigon experience is high-quality and meaningful for everyone who takes part.

We believe that every student should have access to high-quality events where they can learn, build, and share their creations, no matter where in the world they are. As a fully virtual hackathon, we hold ourselves to the Core Values below, which apply to HackSaigon and to everyone who helps run it. By following them, we aim to create the best possible experience for hackers, organizers, mentors, judges, and sponsors alike.

## HackSaigon sets a standard of excellence for attendees

- Have a confirmed schedule, with dates and start and end times published in multiple time zones, that attendees can depend on.
- Run for at least 24 hours of hacking time, with the event platform (chat server, video rooms, and submission site) available and monitored throughout, and a flexible schedule so participants in different time zones can take part fairly.
- Make sure every participant can find the links, schedule, and help they need in one clearly organized place, such as the event website or main server.
- Support participants' wellbeing by encouraging breaks, rest, and healthy habits, and by never expecting anyone to stay online overnight. If food stipends, vouchers, or other perks are offered, make them inclusive of dietary restrictions and different regions.
- Ensure that participants own any intellectual property they produce at the event.
- Clearly communicate any major changes (such as platform, schedule, theme, or judging format) as soon as they occur, through every official channel.
- Verify registrations before the event starts, and keep each participant's name, email, and school or student status up to date, protecting this information carefully.
- Have at least one lead organizer who is available and able to make decisions at any time during the event.
- Provide reliable support, including a help channel and clear instructions for common problems like platform access, video calls, and project submission.

## HackSaigon is open and accessible to all students

- Do not charge entry fees.
- Advertise early enough for hackers around the world to learn about the event and register.
- Do not discriminate on the basis of race, religion, national origin, color, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, social class, economic status, veteran status, disability, or age.
- Make a goodwill effort to advertise the event widely and make the application publicly available.
- Are not secret or invite-only events.
- Have a straightforward, publicly known application process.
- Publish the criteria for admission.
- Publish the rules and regulations for competing, including team size, submission requirements, and judging criteria.
- Design with accessibility in mind, for example by offering captions or transcripts for live sessions, recording workshops when possible, avoiding requirements for cameras or high-bandwidth connections.

## HackSaigon is fair

- Organizers don't compete in the event they are organizing. The same rule applies to judges, sponsors, and other staff.
- The event is open to hackers who are students at the time of the hackathon or who have graduated within the last 12 months. Students include high school students, university undergraduates, postgraduate students. Non-students are welcome as volunteers, mentors, sponsors, and judges.
- Judging is transparent and consistent: all teams are evaluated against the same published criteria, and judges disclose and step back from any conflict of interest.
- Resources, mentor time, and sponsor opportunities are made available to all participants as equally as possible, regardless of time zone.

## HackSaigon is a safe and welcoming space

- Adopt, make available, and enforce the HackSaigon Code of Conduct.
- All organizers agree to the HackSaigon Code of Conduct.
- Have a clear and widely known reporting system for incidents, with an anonymous option.
- Link to the Code of Conduct on the event website, and ask all registrants to agree to it during registration (for example, with a compulsory checkbox).
- Make it clear that the Code of Conduct extends to everyone involved, including hackers, volunteers, organizers, sponsors, judges, mentors, and HackSaigon Staff, across every channel, call, and platform used for the event.
- Make sure that bad actors and attendees who violate the Code of Conduct or event rules are removed from, and do not attend, the event.
- Moderate all event spaces actively, with trained moderators present in chats and live sessions, tools to mute, remove, or restrict disruptive users, and protection against spam, raids, and unwanted direct messages.
- Protect participants' privacy by not recording or sharing anyone's video, voice, or personal information without consent, and by making it clear when sessions are recorded.
- If minors are attending, make sure child safeguarding requirements for the relevant regions are met, including parental or guardian consent where required and extra moderation of any private communication with minors.
- Involve school administrators or faculty where appropriate and helpful, for example when partnering with student clubs or universities.
- Encourage sustainable pacing, including rest and time away from screens, so that hackers of all backgrounds can participate in a healthy way.

## Our priority

Creating an empowering environment where hackers of all skill levels can build amazing things is HackSaigon's main priority. These Community Values describe the kind of event we run and the kind of community we want to build: one that makes a positive contribution to the student hacker movement, wherever its members happen to log in from.
