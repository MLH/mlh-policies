# HackSaigon Hackathon Rules

These are the standard competition rules used at HackSaigon hackathons. Each event's official page (the "Event Page") may add to or modify these rules for that event, and the HackSaigon Contest Terms and Conditions also apply. If anything on the Event Page conflicts with these rules, the Event Page controls for that event. If you have a suggestion for these rules or a question, please email us at contact@hacksaigon.org.

## Spirit of the Competition

Remember that hackathons are like marathons. Some people take part to compete, but most take part to better themselves and have fun. Regardless of why you're at a hackathon, please uphold the hacker spirit and our Community Values by collaborating with other teams, helping beginners, and having fun.

## Hackathon Rules

1. All participants agree to uphold the HackSaigon Code of Conduct to keep every event a safe, welcoming space for everyone.
2. HackSaigon hackathons are primarily for students. Who is eligible is stated on the Event Page. Unless the Event Page says otherwise, we define students broadly: anyone who attends a school, college, or university, or is in a bootcamp or similar program, is a student, and so is anyone who graduated within the last 12 months.
3. Organizers, volunteers, mentors, judges, sponsors, and anyone else in a privileged position at the event should not participate as a hacker.
4. Teams must stay within the team size limits stated on the Event Page, and all team members should actively participate in the event.
5. Teams should make use of advice and support from organizers, volunteers, mentors, sponsors, and each other.
6. All work on a project should be done during the hacking period of the event.
7. Teams can use an idea they had before the event.
8. Teams can work on ideas that have already been done. Hacks do not have to be "innovative." If somebody wants to work on a common idea, they should be allowed to do so and should be judged on the quality of their hack. It is hard to find something fully original, and teams may not know an idea has been done before.
9. Teams can work on an idea that they have worked on before, as long as they do not reuse code or other project materials.
10. Teams can use libraries, frameworks, or open-source code in their projects. Working on a project before the event and open-sourcing it for the sole purpose of using the code during the event is against the spirit of the rules and is not allowed.
11. Teams must stop hacking once the time is up. However, teams may debug and make small fixes to their programs after time is up. For example, if you find a bug while demoing that breaks your application and the fix is only a few lines of code, it's okay to fix it. Making large changes or adding new features is not allowed.
12. Your code must be available publicly (ideally in a git repository).
13. Your code must remain public after the event to be eligible for prizes. If your repository or video is not public, new winners may be selected.
14. Teams can be disqualified from the competition at the organizers' discretion. Reasons may include, but are not limited to, breaking these rules, breaking the Code of Conduct, or other unsporting behavior. Disciplinary action follows our Disciplinary Action Policy.

## AI Tools and Code Generation

Artificial intelligence and large language models have changed how people create applications and write code. HackSaigon encourages our community to learn these technologies and use them. However, it is essential to keep following the spirit of the hackathon. We believe the best projects will make use of AI but will ultimately be created by humans.

1. Teams may use AI to assist them while coding, using tools such as code completion, code generation, image generation, or other similar tools.
2. Teams should be honest and transparent about the AI tools they used. This includes listing them in their project submissions and answering questions about them from organizers, judges, and other hackers.
3. Any additional limits on AI tools for a specific event are stated on the Event Page.

## Online Submission Rules

For online hackathons, and for any event that uses online submissions, the following additional rules apply.

1. All teams must submit a demo video within the length limit stated on the Event Page. Your video must be created during the hacking period of the event.
2. Your video must remain public after the event to be eligible for prizes. If your video is not public, new winners may be selected.
3. Your demo video must state the name of the hackathon at the beginning. For example: "Hey, I'm Sam and this is my demo for \[Event Name\]."
4. You must have completed all registration steps for the hackathon.
5. You are not allowed to submit your project to other hackathons.
6. You may not submit a project that includes prior work.
7. You must submit all other components listed on the Event Page before the submission deadline.

## Demos

After hacking finishes, teams will show their projects to the judges and, where the event allows, to each other. Depending on the event, this may be a prerecorded video, a live online demo, or an in-person demo, as stated on the Event Page.

You are strongly encouraged to demo what you have built. You are judged on what you built, so you only hurt yourself by not showing a demo.

You are encouraged to present what you have done even if your hack is broken or unfinished. It's okay if you didn't finish. That happens all the time, and completion is only one part of the judging criteria, so you might still do well.

Demoing is not just about the competition. It is a chance to share what you learned and what you tried to build. If you have nothing to demo, you can give a short presentation about what you tried and what you learned. Hearing what other people learned is interesting and inspiring for everyone.

## Judging Criteria

Unless the Event Page states different criteria, teams will be judged on these four criteria. Judges will weigh the criteria equally. During judging, participants should try to describe what they did for each criterion in their project.

- **Technology:** How technically impressive was the hack? Was the technical problem the team tackled difficult? Did it use a particularly clever technique or many different components? Did the technology involved make you go "Wow"?
- **Design:** Did the team put thought into the user experience? How well designed is the interface? For a website, this might be about how beautiful the CSS or graphics are. For a hardware project, it might be more about how good the human-computer interaction is (for example, is it easy to use or does it use a cool interface?).
- **Completion:** Does the hack work? Did the team achieve everything they wanted?
- **Learning:** Did the team stretch themselves? Did they try to learn something new? What kind of projects have they worked on before? If a team that always does virtual reality projects decides to switch things up and try a mobile app instead, that exploration should be rewarded.

These criteria will guide judges, but ultimately judges are free to make decisions based on their gut feeling of which projects are the most impressive and most deserving, in line with the Contest Terms and Conditions.

It's important to note that these judging criteria do not include:

- **How good your code is.** It doesn't matter if your code is messy, not well commented, or uses inefficient algorithms. Hacking is about playing around, making mistakes, and learning new things. If your code isn't production ready, you won't be marked down.
- **How well you pitch.** Hacking is about building and learning, not about selling.
- **How good the idea is.** Hackathons aren't about coming up with innovative ideas. They are about building and learning.
- **How well the project solves a problem.** You can build something totally useless and as long as you're learning and having fun, that's a good hack. Sometimes a pointless project is one of the best hacks.

So don't worry about coming up with the next big idea or building the next big thing. You'll have plenty of time for that outside the hackathon. Just focus on learning, having fun, and making new friends.

## Remember!

The competition is just one part of the hackathon. To make the most of the event, try something new, teach others, and make new friends!

Happy Hacking from the HackSaigon team!
