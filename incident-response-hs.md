# HackSaigon Incident Response Guide

The following is a guide of actions to be taken in the case of a Code of Conduct violation at HackSaigon, our fully virtual hackathon.

## Contact HackSaigon Staff

HackSaigon Staff are reachable through the event's communication platform (for example, our Discord server) at all times during the event. Staff members are responsible for addressing violations of the Code of Conduct and following HackSaigon's procedures. If someone comes to you with a concern, politely connect them with HackSaigon Staff for further resolution.

Participants can reach HackSaigon Staff by:

- Sending a private message to any member with the @HackSaigon Staff role
- Using the event's help or incident-reporting channel or ticket system
- Asking to be moved into a private voice or video room during a live session

## Contact the HackSaigon Incident Response Team

If you cannot reach HackSaigon Staff on the event platform, immediately contact the HackSaigon Incident Response Team by email at **contact@hacksaigon.org**. If you do reach a Staff member, they will escalate the report to the Incident Response Team themselves. Anyone may report anonymously.

Below are the rough steps that HackSaigon Staff will go through.

## Ensure Everyone's Safety, Privacy, and Comfort

Our Staff prioritize participant safety over process when necessary. Beyond that, they will do their best to make sure everyone's privacy is respected and that everyone is comfortable throughout the resolution process.

Because the event is online, Staff can take immediate protective steps while a report is reviewed, such as temporarily muting, restricting, or removing the person involved from channels or calls, and removing harmful content.

## Initial Report

The responding Staff member will prepare an initial report of the situation. Depending on the severity and details of the incident, they will follow these guidelines:

- If there is any threat to anyone's safety, or if someone's safety is in doubt, help the person contact local emergency services or law enforcement in their own country, and ask them to do so right away if they are in immediate danger.
- If everyone is presently safe, involve law enforcement or other outside authorities only at the reporter's request.
- Offer the reporter a private space to talk, such as a direct message or a private voice or video room, and let them choose whichever they are most comfortable with. Cameras are never required.
- Explain who they are and ask what happened, so they can help address the situation.
- Provide the reporter with a list of support contacts and emergency resources if they need help later.

Staff will try to get as much of the incident in written form from the reporter as possible. If they cannot, they will transcribe it as it was told to them. The important information to gather includes the following:

- Identifying information of the participant involved, such as their username, display name, and email or account details if known
- The behavior that was in violation
- The approximate time of the behavior, including the time zone
- The circumstances surrounding the incident, including where it happened (channel, call, direct message, or platform)
- Evidence such as screenshots, message links, recordings (where lawfully available), or logs
- Other people involved in the incident
- Other people who know of the incident

Staff should preserve evidence before any content is deleted, and should not ask reporters to confront the person involved.

There are also some guidelines as to what **not** to do as an initial response:

- Do not overtly invite the reporter to withdraw the complaint or mention that withdrawal is OK. This suggests that you want them to do so and is therefore coercive. Saying "if you're OK with pursuing the complaint" suggests that you are by default pursuing it and is not coercive.
- Do not ask the reporter for their advice on how to deal with the complaint, or ask them for input on penalties. This is a Staff responsibility. The responding Staff member will work with the Incident Response Team to respond in a consistent way across the event, and will keep the organizing team involved in the process.

Some things our team can consider when dealing with Code of Conduct offenders can be found in our Disciplinary Action Policy.

## Response

HackSaigon will decide on a course of action with the responding Staff member and the organizers. Staff will ask the person who is the subject of the report to speak privately (by direct message or private call) and summarize the report that was made against them. They will then notify them of HackSaigon's decision.

If the person has a different understanding of the situation, we allow them to give their side of the story, but our course of action will stand until HackSaigon Staff say otherwise.

It is very important how we deal with the incident publicly. Our policy is to make sure that everyone aware of the initial incident is also made aware that it is not according to policy and that official action has been taken, while still respecting the privacy of individual participants. When speaking to individuals who were aware of the incident but were not directly involved, we will leave out specific details.

Depending on the incident, the Incident Response Team, in coordination with the responding Staff member, will determine if any public announcements are necessary, such as a message in the event's announcement channel or a note during a live session. Staff are the primary communicators for such announcements, and any announcement will be agreed on by the organizing team with careful attention to its wording and timing.

If participants express distress or anger about an incident and its handling:

- Acknowledge their feelings and sincerely express regret that their experience was negatively affected by the incident.
- Reassure them that such matters are taken seriously and that appropriate steps are being taken or have been taken, respecting the privacy of those involved.
- If they wish to provide further feedback or discuss their concerns in more detail, offer them clear channels to do so by speaking further with HackSaigon Staff when appropriate, or encouraging them to provide written feedback by email to **contact@hacksaigon.org** so their concerns can be addressed.
